How the model works

Returns

Each simulated year draws real stock and bond returns. In lognormal mode, log-returns are bivariate normal and correlated through a Cholesky factor: zb = ρzs + √(1−ρ²)·z₂. The log-space parameters, including ρ, are solved so that the arithmetic means, standard deviations and correlation match the inputs exactly. Fat-tail mode is a two-regime mixture: 10% of years have twice the volatility, for stocks and bonds together, with the same mean and overall volatility, so crashes are more frequent.

Spending

Fees are charged on assets each year. Withdrawals come out at the start of each year in today’s dollars, so the fixed rule is the inflation-adjusted “4% rule” framing. A path fails in the first year the balance cannot cover that year’s withdrawal, or if spending ever falls below 80% of the starting amount. Guardrails follow a simplified Guyton–Klinger rule: if the current withdrawal rate drifts 20% above the starting rate, spending is cut 10% (skipped in the final 15 years); if it drifts 20% below, spending rises 10%.

Estimation

Success probability is p̂ = successes ⁄ N, with standard error √(p̂(1−p̂)⁄N) and a 95% Wilson score interval, which stays honest even when every path succeeds. With antithetic variates on, the SE and interval come from the variance of pair averages over N⁄2 pairs. The interval covers sampling error only, not uncertainty about the assumptions themselves. A seeded generator (Mulberry32 with Box–Muller normals) makes every run reproducible.

Not modeled

  • Taxes, Social Security, pensions and annuity income
  • Mortality: the horizon is fixed, not a life expectancy
  • Mean reversion, regimes and inflation shocks: years are independent and identically distributed
  • Spending changes over retirement beyond the guardrail rule
