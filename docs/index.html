How the model works

Where the numbers come from

This page has no finance code of its own. It sends your inputs to retirement_mc/web.py, the repository's Python package running in your browser with Pyodide, and draws what comes back. With the same seed, every number matches running the package on a laptop (NumPy's PCG64 generator); the default inputs reproduce the README's 89.4% for the 4% rule.

Returns

Each simulated year draws real stock and bond returns. Lognormal mode makes log-returns bivariate normal, with parameters solved so the arithmetic means, volatilities and correlation match the inputs exactly. Fat tails is a two-regime mixture: 10% of years have twice the volatility, with the same mean and overall volatility. History stitches together random 5-year blocks of actual 1928–2025 returns.

Income and lifespans

Guaranteed income covers part of spending; the portfolio covers the rest. Social Security follows current claiming rules (70% of the full benefit at 62, 124% at 70). Random lifespans follow the Gompertz law of mortality, and annuities are priced from the same survival curve with a 5% insurer load. With random lifespans, success means every year you were alive was funded.

Estimation

Success probability is p̂ = successes ⁄ N, with a 95% Wilson score interval that stays honest even when every path succeeds. The interval covers sampling error only, not uncertainty about the assumptions, which the sensitivity chart and the alternative-assumptions line address. Comparisons, the heatmap and the sensitivity chart use common random numbers, so their differences are not sampling noise.

Not modeled

  • Taxes, required minimum distributions and account types
  • Spousal and survivor benefits; one retiree only
  • Inflation shocks and regimes: inflation is constant and returns are independent from year to year (except in History mode)
  • Spending changes over retirement beyond the spending rules
